How To Get Abs

A Fat Loss Guide For Men

This is a nothing more, nothing less breakdown on how to see visible abs

This was made for men who presumably have at least 10-20lbs+ of fat covering their abs and have never seen them before.

This is also curated such that a man who knows nothing about health and fitness understands and can take action from this breakdown alone.

Time is valuable, so I tried to keep this as succinct as possible, without sacrificing necessary info or context.

My personal objective put another way: Save you an hour of trying to accumulate info on ChatGPT, and years of trial and error trying to do this on your own.

01

How fat loss works:

Fat loss is simple: your body stores fat as extra energy, and it loses fat only when it needs more energy than you give it from food.

This is called a "calorie deficit", where you are consuming less calories than you are burning on a daily or weekly basis.

When you consistently eat slightly less energy than your body uses, it is forced to pull from stored fat to survive.

If that doesn't happen, fat loss doesn't happen — no matter how "clean" you eat or how hard you train. You can lose weight while only eating McDonalds, and gain weight only eating chicken and broccoli. So the ideal situation is eating both the quantity of calories that will generate fat loss, and the quality of calories that will make you energized and healthy.

02

You Cannot Spot Reduce

Your body doesn't choose where fat comes off first and for most men, stomach fat is not only the last to go, but also the hardest to shed.

This means that doing ab exercises does NOTHING to burn the fat that's on top of your abs, except burn a few calories, helping you get into a calorie deficit, which will initiate fat loss across your body as a whole, and again your body decides what fat is shed first etc.

Doing ab exercises will help build your abs, like any other muscle. Which means that as you shed fat through a calorie deficit, the more you build your abs, the sooner they will be visible, and the more pronounced they'll be at the end.

The fat that's covering your abs is usually your last 5-10lbs, and it's certainly harder to shed than your first 5-10lbs.

03

Genetic Factors

Genetics influence how fat loss looks, not whether fat loss happens. Everyone loses fat the same way (by being in a calorie deficit), but genetics determine where your body prefers to store fat and where it lets it go last.

For most men, again the stomach is the most stubborn area, which is why abs are usually the final thing to show, even when the rest of the body looks lean. Some men will see abs at higher body fat levels, while others must get leaner before they're visible.

Genetics also affect things like natural muscle shape, ab symmetry, and how sharply defined your abs appear once lean.

What genetics do not do is prevent you from getting abs entirely. There's no such thing as "I can't get abs because of my genetics". If you consistently lose fat while maintaining muscle, your abs will show eventually.

The only difference genetics creates is how long it takes and how lean you must become in order to see them, not whether the outcome is possible.

04

The Anatomy of the Abs.

There are two main parts, your abs (In the middle), and obliques (on the outside).

Both contribute to a nice overall mid section look.

You are going to want to do exercises that target the abs 50-75% of the time, and exercises that target the obliques 25-50% of the time.

Can also break the abs into the upper and lower abs. The lower abs specifically will be the last to be revealed, and hardest to build.

05

Ab exercises

You don't need a fancy routine, you don't need to be doing hanging leg raises from a bar while some dude punches you in the stomach between each rep.

Many men are stumped on which ab exercises to do, when in fact the first 3-5 that come to their mind off the top of their head would likely be sufficient, they just don't do them.

All the exercises that will help you build abs are pretty simple, such as leg raises, sit ups, V-Tucks, Side planks, Side plank raises, Bicycle kicks, and Russian twists etc.

Those exercises comprise 96% of the targeted core work I've ever done.

06

How much and how often

Hitting abs once per week will move you in the right direction. Twice per week would move you faster. Hitting them 3 times a week would build them faster. But it depends on how fast your body can recover.

If you're really only starting to attack abs for the first time, twice per week, with 8-10 total sets will be sufficient to build them.

Again the main ab revealing mechanism here is going to be the calorie deficit, whether you hit abs once or three times per week.

07

Core Engagement during other movements

It's important to engage your core during other movements in the gym, especially movements where you want to stabilize your back like squats and deadlifts, but ultimately you should be actively engaging your core every time you pick up a dumbbell, and in almost any other movement in the gym.

This will not only keep you safe in the gym, but also in life as well, because developing the habit of bracing your core is important whether you are picking up a dumbbell or a baby.

And yes, all that core bracing will contribute to building your abs over time as well, not as much as direct ab exercises, but it certainly helps.

08

Other Factors that contribute to Fat Loss:

Food controls fat loss because it's easy to eat more energy than you burn, while exercise mainly helps by preserving muscle and keeping your body functioning well.

Protein matters because it basically tells your body to burn fat instead of muscle. A good amount of protein to eat per day is 1g of protein per 1lbs of bodyweight (or more optimally GOAL bodyweight)

Walking and daily movement increase energy use without stressing your system.

Sleep matters because poor sleep increases hunger and makes it harder to stay in a deficit. Everything in the body functions better when you're rested, and worse when your not.

09

The Maintenance of abs

Maintaining visible abs is about staying close to your lean threshold.

Once you have abs, small increases in body fat can blur them quickly, especially for men whose genetics store fat in the stomach first and quickly.

It can be futile to try to maintain sharp abs year round. This is why many people move through cut and bulk cycles.

A cut is a period where you intentionally eat slightly less to reduce fat and reveal abs. A bulk is a period where you eat slightly more to build muscle, knowing that some fat gain is normal and abs may soften or disappear temporarily.

Note: A lean bulk is a type of calorie surplus where you are eating barely MORE calories than you are burning, such that you gain weight and muscle slowly, while keeping your BF% gain as low as possible.

Once our clients shed the fat and reach the muscle gain phase, this is the approach we typically take.

Some men can easily maintain at least slightly visible abs during a lean bulk, and some men must accept that they aren't really going to see their abs during bulk season.

And either way, it's okay, you'll have the tools to reveal the abs again at any point.

The goal is not perfection, but control. Staying within a range where fat gain is intentional and reversible.

10
